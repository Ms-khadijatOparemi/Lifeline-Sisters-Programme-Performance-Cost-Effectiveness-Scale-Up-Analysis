# Lifeline-Sisters-Programme-Performance-Cost-Effectiveness-Scale-Up-Analysis

# Project Overview 
The Lifeline Sisters: Programme Performance, Cost-Effectiveness &amp; Scale-Up Analysis project examines the performance and operational efficiency of a fictional community-based sexual and reproductive health programme in Nigeria.

The programme model involves trained women, referred to as Lifeline Sisters, providing contraceptive counselling, information, referrals and selected contraceptive services within their communities.

The purpose of this analysis is to prepare a structured evidence base for programme decision-making, including understanding programme performance over time, identifying differences across locations, examining cost drivers and unit economics, assessing the relationship between training and performance, and evaluating options for future programme expansion.

The project is designed to demonstrate an end-to-end analytical workflow, from raw data inspection and quality assessment through data modelling, exploratory analysis, cost-effectiveness modelling and ultimately an evidence-based scale-up recommendation.


# Your role
You are a Strategy & Impact Analyst supporting the MEL team.
You are a Strategy & Impact Analyst at a fictional Nigerian reproductive health organisation called Lifeline Health Initiative (LHI) supporting the MEL team


# Scenerio
LHI operates a community-based contraception programme through trained community health workers called Lifeline Sisters. 
Leadership is considering investing additional funding of N50 million in its programme and is also considering changing how Lifeline Sisters are trained. Using programme, cost, training, outcome and external evidence data, assess programme performance, identify the key drivers of cost and impact, model alternative scenarios, and recommend how LHI should proceed.


Should LHI expand the programme, and if so, which expansion model appears to offer the best value for money?



# Analytical Questions

The analysis is being developed around the following decision questions:

How has programme performance changed over time?

How does performance vary across states and programme locations?

What factors appear to be associated with higher programme productivity?

What are the major programme cost drivers?

What does programme performance look like relative to the resources invested?

How do training type and training history relate to programme performance?

How does contraceptive stock availability affect programme activity?

What are the estimated programme outcomes and associated costs?

What assumptions and uncertainties materially affect the cost-effectiveness estimates?

If ₦50 million were available for expansion, where and how should it be allocated?

The analysis is intended to distinguish between observed programme data, modelled outcomes and assumptions, rather than presenting estimates as directly observed facts.
